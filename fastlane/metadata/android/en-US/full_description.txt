Turns white-paper-black-ink pages into black-paper-white-ink after dark, on BOOX
e-ink tablets — without giving up the reader you already use.

Every standard way of inverting an Android screen is inert on these devices. The
accessibility "Colour inversion" toggle, the SurfaceFlinger colour matrix and the
daltonizer all accept the call and leave the panel unchanged, because the E-Ink
display path never applies the transform. Onyx's own per-app image settings have no
inversion of any kind.

So Inkverse produces already-inverted pixels as ordinary app content. It screenshots
the window in front, runs it through a colour matrix, and draws the result in a
fullscreen overlay that pen and touch pass straight through. Your reader keeps
working exactly as it did — annotations, stylus, dictionary, everything — only the
pixels change.

Modes:

* Grayscale invert — the right choice on Kaleido colour e-ink, where inverting RGB
  produces muddy casts
* RGB invert — keeps hue; photographs become negatives
* Grayscale invert with black and white point levels, for scans with a heavy grey
  background
* Passthrough, for checking the pipeline is neutral

The overlay hides itself while you are in Inkverse, so its own controls stay
readable, and comes back when you leave.

Needs the accessibility permission for two specific reasons, both structural: only a
window owned by an accessibility service may be fully opaque and non-touchable at
the same time, and per-window screenshots are the only capture on these devices that
cannot end up capturing the overlay itself.

No dependencies, no analytics, no network access except the optional update check
against GitHub Releases, which can be turned off.
